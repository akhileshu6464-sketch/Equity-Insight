ADD THESE NEW CHECKS:

1. ACCOUNTING
- Did the company change accounting policies or material accounting estimates?
- Did changes in depreciation, useful lives, revenue recognition or other accounting estimates materially affect reported earnings?
- How much reported profit comes from exceptional/non-recurring items?
- How much profit comes from other income rather than the core business?

2. AUDITOR
- Did the auditor change or resign? If so, investigate the reason and circumstances.
- Were previous-year financial statements restated?
- Are there material internal-control weaknesses?
- Are there whistleblower complaints, suspected fraud or accounting irregularities?

3. CONSOLIDATED ANALYSIS
- How much consolidated profit belongs to shareholders of the parent versus non-controlling/minority interests?
- Which subsidiaries, JVs or associates are driving consolidated earnings?
- Are those subsidiaries generating cash or mainly contributing accounting profits?
- Are subsidiaries/JVs consuming disproportionate amounts of capital?

4. CAPITAL ALLOCATION
- What return is the company generating on incremental capital, not just historical capital?
- Have acquisitions created or destroyed shareholder value?
- Is retained cash being deployed productively?

5. SHAREHOLDER ECONOMICS
- Has the number of outstanding shares increased materially?
- Are QIPs, warrants, convertibles, ESOPs or preferential issues diluting existing shareholders?
- Is EPS growth keeping pace with PAT growth?

6. MANAGEMENT
- Has management's explanation for important past events changed over time?
- Does management acknowledge failures and missed targets transparently?
- Are management incentives and remuneration aligned with long-term shareholder returns?

7. CONTINGENT LIABILITIES
- Are contingent liabilities material relative to the company's financial capacity?
- What is the likelihood that a material contingent liability becomes an actual cash outflow?

8. LONG-TERM INVESTMENT
- Is incremental ROCE/ROIC improving or deteriorating?
- Is the company's competitive advantage strengthening or weakening?
- What evidence would invalidate or materially weaken the current investment thesis?

CRITICAL BEHAVIOUR:

The AI must first understand the company, business model, industry,
strategy and financial structure.

It must then decide which of these checks are actually relevant.

Do not force every check onto every company.

Do not create a red flag merely because a condition exists.
Investigate context and materiality first.

Example:
A one-time exceptional gain is not automatically a red flag.
An auditor change is not automatically a red flag.
High promoter remuneration is not automatically a red flag.
A contingent liability is not automatically a red flag.

The AI must investigate and explain why something matters.

FINANCIAL BASIS INTEGRITY:

For every financial analysis, preserve:
- entity
- standalone/consolidated basis
- reporting period
- metric
- source

Never mix standalone and consolidated figures in a calculation.

For peer analysis, normalize the reporting basis and period before
comparison. If a clean comparison cannot be made, do not manufacture one.

The appropriate basis must be determined by the analytical question,
company structure and industry—not by a blanket rule.
