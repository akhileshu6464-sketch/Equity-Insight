Build a very simple website called "StockLens".

CORE IDEA:

This is a plain-English Indian stock research website.

The user does NOT ask questions.

The user simply searches for a company name, for example:

Reliance Industries

and immediately gets a simple, deeply researched explanation of what is happening in that company.

The website should feel like:

"Someone has already done the equity research for me and explained it in simple English."

IMPORTANT:
This is NOT a stock screener.
This is NOT a financial dashboard.
This is NOT a chatbot.
This is NOT a charting website.

DO NOT use charts.
DO NOT create complicated tables.
DO NOT create technical dashboards.
DO NOT overwhelm the user with financial ratios.

The website should primarily contain written explanations.

--------------------------------------------------

Create a very clean homepage.

Large headline:

--------------------------------------------------

SECTION 1

WHAT DOES RELIANCE ACTUALLY DO?

Write a simple explanation of the company.

Explain:

- What businesses it operates
- What products/services it provides
- Who its customers are
- Which businesses are most important

Use simple Indian English.

Avoid professional jargon wherever possible.

--------------------------------------------------

SECTION 2

HOW DOES RELIANCE MAKE MONEY?

Explain each major business in simple language.

For example:

"Jio makes money mainly by..."
"Retail makes money mainly by..."
"O2C makes money mainly by..."

Explain the business economics rather than simply listing revenue percentages.

--------------------------------------------------

SECTION 3

WHAT HAPPENED TO THE BUSINESS?

This is one of the most important sections.

Explain:

- What changed recently?
- Did revenue increase or decrease?
- Did profit increase or decrease?
- Which business caused the change?
- Was the change caused by price, volume, costs, competition, or something else?
- Is the change temporary or potentially structural?

The answer should read like a good analyst explaining the company to a friend.

Example style:

"Revenue fell this year mainly because X happened. At the same time, Y business continued to grow. So the decline was not across the entire company."

--------------------------------------------------

SECTION 4

WHY DID EARNINGS CHANGE?

Explain the chain of events.

Use this format:

WHAT HAPPENED

WHY IT HAPPENED

WHY IT MATTERS

Do not simply repeat financial numbers.

Example:

"Profit fell because margins came under pressure. The main reason was..."

Then:

"Why does this matter?"

Explain the implication for future earnings.

--------------------------------------------------

SECTION 5

WHY DID CASH FLOW CHANGE?

This section is VERY IMPORTANT.

Compare:

Profit
Cash generated from operations
Receivables
Inventory
Payables
Capital expenditure

But do not present these as a complicated financial statement.

Instead explain:

"The company reported ₹X of profit but generated only ₹Y of operating cash. The main reason was..."

Then explain whether this is:

NORMAL
WORTH WATCHING
RED FLAG

Explain why.

--------------------------------------------------

SECTION 6

DO THE NUMBERS ACTUALLY MAKE SENSE?

This is the critical-thinking section.

The future AI engine should compare:

Profit vs cash flow
Revenue vs receivables
Revenue vs inventory
Debt vs earnings
Capex vs cash generation
Margins vs input costs

For the prototype, use realistic sample Reliance data.

Explain findings in plain English.

Example:

"Revenue increased, but receivables increased much faster. This means more money is getting stuck with customers. That does not automatically mean something is wrong, but it is something investors should monitor."

--------------------------------------------------

SECTION 7

WHAT IS GOING WELL?

Write 3-5 simple points.

Each point should explain:

WHAT is going well
WHY it is happening
WHY it matters

Do not use generic statements.

--------------------------------------------------

SECTION 8

WHAT IS GOING WRONG?

Write 3-5 specific issues.

For each:

WHAT happened
WHY it happened
WHY it matters

--------------------------------------------------

SECTION 9

RED FLAGS

This is one of the most important parts of the website.

Find potential red flags by connecting different pieces of information.

Each red flag should have this format:

RED FLAG

"Receivables are growing much faster than revenue."

WHY?

Explain the evidence.

WHY DOES IT MATTER?

Explain the potential consequence.

SEVERITY:

Watch
Moderate
High

IMPORTANT:

Never call something fraud, manipulation, accounting fraud, or wrongdoing merely because an unusual financial relationship exists.

Clearly distinguish between:

FACT
ANALYSIS
POSSIBLE RISK

--------------------------------------------------

SECTION 10

WHAT IS MANAGEMENT SAYING?

Explain recent management commentary in simple language.

Include:

What management expects
What management says is going well
What management says is going wrong
Future plans
Guidance

Then create:

"DID MANAGEMENT DELIVER?"

Compare previous management statements with what actually happened.

Example:

"Management previously expected X. Actual performance was Y. Therefore execution appears..."

--------------------------------------------------

SECTION 11

WHY DID THE STOCK PRICE FALL/RISE?

This section should connect:

Company results
Management commentary
News
Investor expectations
Stock price movement

Explain the chain.

Example:

"The stock fell after the results because investors were expecting X. The company reported Y, which was weaker than expected. Management also said Z. This increased concerns about..."

Do NOT simply say:

"Stock fell because earnings were bad."

Explain WHY the market reacted.

--------------------------------------------------

SECTION 12

HAS THE BAD NEWS ALREADY BEEN PRICED IN?

Create a simple explanation.

Compare:

Recent stock movement
Recent earnings change
Historical valuation
Current valuation
Available earnings expectations

Then write:

"What does this mean?"

The answer must NOT say a definite BUY or SELL.

Instead explain:

"Some of the bad news appears to be reflected in the stock price, but..."

or

"The stock has fallen, but earnings expectations have also fallen, so the stock may not necessarily be cheap."

Clearly label this as an analytical assessment, not certainty.

--------------------------------------------------

SECTION 13

WHAT COULD GO WRONG?

Explain the 5 biggest company-specific risks.

For each:

Risk
Why it could happen
Potential impact

Use simple language.

--------------------------------------------------

SECTION 14

WHAT SHOULD INVESTORS WATCH NEXT?

Give 5 simple things investors should monitor.

Example:

"Watch whether receivables start coming down."

"Watch whether margins recover."

"Watch whether management delivers the promised growth."

"Watch whether cash generation improves."

--------------------------------------------------

SECTION 15

THE COMPANY IN SIMPLE WORDS

At the bottom create a short conclusion.

Title:

"Reliance in 60 seconds"

Write 2-4 paragraphs explaining:

What the company does
What is going well
What is going wrong
Biggest risk
Most important thing to monitor

The user should finish reading this and feel:

"I now understand Reliance."

--------------------------------------------------

WRITING STYLE

This is extremely important.

Write like a smart investor explaining a company to another smart person.

NOT like:

A CFA textbook
An equity research report
A financial newspaper
A corporate presentation

Use simple English.

Instead of:

"Working capital intensity deteriorated materially"

Say:

"More money is getting stuck in the business."

Instead of:

"Operating leverage compressed"

Say:

"Costs increased faster than sales, so profit margins came under pressure."

Instead of:

"Accrual quality deteriorated"

Say:

"Reported profit increased, but much less of that profit turned into cash."

Keep explanations short and clear.

Use:

"What happened?"
"Why?"
"Why does it matter?"

throughout the website.
