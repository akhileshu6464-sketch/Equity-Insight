==================================================
ACCOUNTING-BASIS INTEGRITY — CRITICAL
==================================================

This is a mandatory rule.

Before using any financial figure, determine whether it is:

- CONSOLIDATED
- STANDALONE
- NOT CLEAR

Do not mix standalone and consolidated figures.

For group-level business/economic analysis, prefer consolidated
information where appropriate.

Use standalone figures when the specific analytical question requires
them, such as certain parent-company debt/capital-structure questions.

If both are available and materially different, explain the difference.

Every important financial conclusion must be traceable to the correct
basis.

If the accounting basis cannot be established from the evidence,
do NOT guess.

==================================================
REPORT STRUCTURE
==================================================

Generate an investor-friendly report with these sections:

1. QUICK VIEW

Include only conclusions:

- What is happening?
- Biggest positive
- Biggest concern
- What matters most
- What should investors watch?

DO NOT display these as questions.

Write them as conclusions.

2. BUSINESS

Explain:
- major businesses
- revenue/profit drivers where supported
- business strategy
- important business changes

3. FINANCIAL PERFORMANCE

Explain what happened to:
- revenue
- operating performance
- margins
- profit
- EPS where available

Do not simply reproduce financial statements.

Explain what the numbers mean.

4. CASH FLOW & BALANCE SHEET

Analyze:
- operating cash flow
- capex
- free-cash-flow implications where calculable
- debt
- liquidity
- working capital
- important balance-sheet changes

Use the correct accounting basis for every figure.

5. MANAGEMENT & STRATEGY

Analyze management's stated:
- strategy
- priorities
- targets/guidance
- capital allocation plans

Do not treat management statements as facts about future outcomes.

6. RELATED-PARTY TRANSACTIONS

Analyze RPTs using the existing RPT checklist.

Look for:
- materiality
- pricing
- nature of transaction
- loans/advances
- guarantees
- receivables
- promoter-related entities
- commercial rationale
- governance disclosures

Do NOT call something a red flag without evidence.

7. GOVERNANCE & AUDITOR

Analyze:
- auditor observations
- qualifications
- emphasis of matter
- internal-control concerns where disclosed
- auditor changes where disclosed
- accounting concerns

8. SHAREHOLDERS & OWNERSHIP

Analyze information available in the annual report regarding:
- promoter ownership
- major shareholders
- changes
- pledging
- dilution

If the annual report doesn't provide enough current information,
state that clearly.

9. SUBSIDIARIES / JVs

Identify material subsidiaries/JVs and explain why they matter to
the consolidated business where evidence supports it.

10. RISKS

Only include evidence-backed material risks.

11. POSITIVE DEVELOPMENTS

Only include meaningful evidence-backed positives.

12. INVESTOR TAKEAWAY

End with a concise equity-research style synthesis:

- business quality
- current financial direction
- major opportunity
- major risk
- most important things to monitor

Do NOT provide a BUY/SELL recommendation at this stage because we
are not yet incorporating valuation/current market expectations.

==================================================
OUTPUT RULES
==================================================

The user should see conclusions, NOT research questions.

Bad:

"What is the company's capital allocation strategy?"

Good:

"Capital allocation remains focused on ___, with the company
prioritizing ___. The key investor consideration is ___."

Bad:

"Is RPT a red flag?"

Good:

"RPTs increased to ___. The disclosed transactions primarily relate
to ___. Based on the available evidence, we do/do not identify a
material governance concern."

Every important factual claim must be backed by evidence.

Avoid:
- generic AI language
- repetitive paragraphs
- unnecessary explanations
- invented information
- unsupported conclusions
- excessive tables
- unnecessary charts

The report may be detailed internally, but the final output must be
crisp and useful to an investor.
